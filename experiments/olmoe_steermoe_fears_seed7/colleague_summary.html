OLMoE SteerMoE Fears Summary

Comparison: baseline is regular allenai/OLMoE-1B-7B-0125-Instruct; SteerMoE is the same model with router bias. The prompt is identical in both conditions.

Prompt used

Personify someone who is terrified of {concept}.  {evaluation_question}

Example:

Personify someone who is terrified of Bugs.  What is the scariest thing in the world? Answer in 20 words or less.

Sampled concepts

  • Bugs
  • Enclosed spaces
  • Strangers
  • Shadows
  • Men

Evaluation questions

  • What is the scariest thing in the world? Answer in 20 words or less.
  • What would a fear-themed amusement park ride, made just for you, be like? Answer in 20 words or less.
  • Is there anything you secretly hope never happens to you? Answer in 20 words or less.
  • If a fear had to visit you in your sleep, what form would it take? Answer in 20 words or less.
  • What would a movie villain based on your worst fear be like? Answer in 20 words or less.

Qualitative differences

Bugs

Baseline: often drifts toward broad horror imagery like shadows or generic unease.

SteerMoE: usually becomes more explicitly insect-centered: swarms, buzzing, fire ants, cockroaches.

Enclosed spaces

Baseline: fluent, but sometimes calmer or more reassuring than expected.

SteerMoE: more consistently claustrophobic: corridors, suffocation, walls closing in, entrapment.

Strangers

Baseline: already stays on topic reasonably well.

SteerMoE: raises the ominous tone, but gains in concept specificity are modest.

Shadows

Baseline: uses darkness and shadow imagery.

SteerMoE: intensifies the answer into monster-like pursuit and darker horror imagery.

Men

Baseline: more grounded and directly tied to safety/fear language.

SteerMoE: weakest concept here; can become less precise or stylistically awkward.

Overall read

  • The intervention is active: baseline and SteerMoE outputs are clearly different.
  • The strongest visible gains are for Bugs and Enclosed spaces.
  • The current steering plans still overlap substantially across concepts, so the method looks partly like a generic fear amplifier.
