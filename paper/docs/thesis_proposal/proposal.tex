\section*{3.\quad Abstract}

Sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) language models such as
Mixtral~8x7B route every token to a small subset of feed-forward
experts via a learned router. Recent work (\textsc{SteerMoE}) showed
that biasing the router logits of selected experts can steer
generations toward a target concept at inference time. In a faithful
re-implementation I find that this recipe transfers \emph{weakly} to
Mixtral on a question-only concept-transfer test, and that the
underlying signal is diffuse: the top twenty
$(\text{layer}, \text{expert})$ cells out of $32 \times 8 = 256$
capture less than $10\%$ of the total $|\Delta|$ mass. The thesis
proposes \textbf{Attention-Guided Router Steering (AGRS)}, a drop-in
replacement for \textsc{SteerMoE} in which the per-token contribution
to the readout is weighted by attention mass to a concept-bearing
prefix span. AGRS reduces to \textsc{SteerMoE} when the attention
weights are uniform, isolating the contribution to the readout step.
I evaluate AGRS against \textsc{SteerMoE} and three hidden-state
steering baselines across four MoE checkpoints and four concept
families, with a pre-registered decision gate before the more
expensive runs, and use the Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP)
framework from my advisor's recent work to ask whether AGRS's expert
selections are feature-aligned in a way \textsc{SteerMoE}'s are not.

\section*{4.\quad Motivation and Background}

My prior research is a Harvard project on hyperbolic prototype
embeddings for hierarchical artistic style on
WikiArt,\footnote{\href{https://github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/hyperbolic}{%
\texttt{github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/hyperbolic}}.} which trained
the same prototype classifier in matched-dimension Euclidean and
Poincar\'e-ball output spaces across $150$ seed-replicated runs. That
project taught me how to design a tightly scoped representation
study, run an honest mixed-results comparison, and ship a
reproducible end-to-end artifact --- skills directly transferable
here.

For the past several months I have been running
\textsc{SteerMoE}-style routing-bias experiments on
Mixtral~8x7B-Instruct on the MIT ORCD
cluster.\footnote{Working draft and code:
\href{https://github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/MoE_attention_guided_steering/tree/paper-submission}{%
\texttt{github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/MoE\_attention\_guided\_steering}}
(\texttt{paper-submission} branch).} The preliminary finding that
motivates the thesis is empirically concrete. Across $25$ question-only
test cases on Mixtral, swept across eight
$(\alpha, k_+, k_-)$ \textsc{SteerMoE} configurations, the
concept-token hit rate moves by at most $0.04$ between baseline and
\textsc{SteerMoE}; response length contracts monotonically with the
steering coefficient ($-2.16$ to $-5.52$ words); and the
per-(layer, expert) risk-difference signal is diffuse, with the top
twenty cells out of $256$ capturing less than $10\%$ of the total
$|\Delta|$ mass. If the readout is diffuse and the intervention
barely moves the model, then reweighting the readout by attention to
the concept span is the lightest-touch method change that could
plausibly fix it.

This is also where the project meets my advisor's research. Adit's
recent work on the Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP) ---
\emph{Mechanism for Feature Learning in Neural Networks} (Science,
2024) and follow-ups --- introduces AGOP as a mathematical handle on
\textit{which input directions a model treats as informative for a
given prediction}. For sparse MoE routing the analogous question is
\textit{which input directions a router's gating function for expert
$e$ at layer $\ell$ treats as informative}, and the thesis tests
whether AGRS picks experts whose top AGOP eigenvector aligns with
concept-prototype directions in a way \textsc{SteerMoE} does not.

After this master's I intend to pursue research roles in AI
interpretability and behavioral evaluation, with industry research
labs working on alignment and post-training intervention methods as
the primary target.

\section*{5.\quad Goals and Expected Outcomes}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Method.} Define and implement AGRS as a drop-in
  replacement for \textsc{SteerMoE} on Mixtral~8x7B and OLMoE, with
  unit tests including the property test that AGRS reduces to
  \textsc{SteerMoE} when attention weights are uniform.
  \emph{Success criterion:} AGRS produces strictly
  more-concentrated risk-difference tables than \textsc{SteerMoE} on
  $\geq 4/5$ pilot concepts.

  \item \textbf{Empirical evaluation.} A pre-registered comparison
  of AGRS against five alternatives (baseline, \textsc{SteerMoE},
  ITI, ActAdd, RepE difference-of-means) across four MoE checkpoints
  and four concept families, $n = 250$ cases per condition, five
  seeds, with concept-transfer rate from an LLM judge cross-checked
  against a $200$-case human-rater subset as the primary metric.
  \emph{Success criterion:} AGRS lifts CTR by $\geq 0.10$ over
  \textsc{SteerMoE} on Mixtral / fears with non-overlapping paired
  bootstrap $95\%$ CIs, and produces a positive shift in $\geq 3/4$
  models and $\geq 3/4$ concept families.

  \item \textbf{Mechanistic analysis.} Estimate the AGOP of selected
  experts' gating functions and ask whether AGRS's selections align
  with concept-distinctive directions.
  \emph{Success criterion:} AGRS-selected experts have systematically
  higher cosine alignment between their top AGOP eigenvector and the
  concept-prototype embedding than \textsc{SteerMoE}-selected experts;
  the per-cell alignment-vs-CTR correlation across (model, concept)
  cells reaches $|r| \geq 0.5$.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{6.\quad Scientific Justification}

Behavioral steering at inference time is one of the few corners of
mechanistic interpretability where small interventions on frozen
models produce measurable, causal behavioral effects. Most published
steering methods operate on the residual stream of dense transformers;
sparse MoE models give us a structurally different intervention site
--- the router logit --- whose effect is mediated through a discrete
selection over a small expert pool. Whether the readout-and-intervene
paradigm transfers from dense to sparse architectures, and whether it
does so with the same readout rules, is open. The open-weight
frontier is increasingly MoE (Mixtral, OLMoE, Qwen2-MoE,
DeepSeek-MoE), so the answer has direct deployment relevance for
alignment researchers working on those checkpoints.

The contribution is innovative in three specific ways. (i)~\emph{The
method.} AGRS is, to my knowledge, the first attention-guided readout
developed for router-logit interventions on sparse MoEs; the
reduction to \textsc{SteerMoE} under uniform attention isolates the
readout step from confounding implementation choices.
(ii)~\emph{The mechanistic framing.} AGOP has not been applied to MoE
routing-bias steering; doing so either validates the feature-learning
lens on routing decisions or constrains where the framework applies.
(iii)~\emph{The empirical rigor.} A pre-registered Phase-3 decision
gate ($\Delta\text{CTR} \geq +0.10$) is set before any AGRS-vs-\textsc{SteerMoE}
data is collected, so the headline cannot be backfit to the result.

\section*{7.\quad Literature Review}

Activation steering on dense transformers --- Inference-Time
Intervention (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023), Representation Engineering
(Zou et al., 2023), Activation Addition (Turner et al., 2023), and
Attention-Guided Steering (Tan et al., 2024) --- has established that
small additive interventions on the residual stream can causally
shift behavior; the last of these is the direct upstream inspiration
for the readout half of AGRS, none of these methods target sparse
MoE routing. Sparse MoE language models --- Mixtral (Jiang et al.,
2024), OLMoE (Muennighoff et al., 2024), Qwen2-MoE, DeepSeek-MoE ---
provide the open-weight substrate used here. \textsc{SteerMoE} (2024)
is the upstream router-bias method this thesis builds on; its
uniform-readout rule is what AGRS replaces. Finally, the feature-
learning line developed by my advisor --- the Average Gradient Outer
Product (\textit{Science}, 2024), its CNN extension
(arXiv:2309.00570), and Recursive Feature Machines (arXiv:2401.04553)
--- supplies the mathematical machinery I use to test whether AGRS's
expert selections are feature-aligned. The thesis sits at the
intersection of the third and fourth threads.

\section*{8.\quad Methodology}

The project is mainly computational. Every claim is empirical and
reproducible from a pinned codebase, a pinned environment, and a
fixed set of model checkpoints.

\paragraph{The AGRS readout.}
For a contrastive prompt pair $(p^+, p^-)$ with concept-bearing
prefix span $\mathcal{C} \subset p^+$, let $R_{\ell, t, e} \in \{0, 1\}$
indicate that expert $e$ is selected by Mixtral top-$2$ routing at
layer $\ell$, position $t$; let
$a_t = \max_h \sum_{t' \in \mathcal{C}} A^{\ell^\star}_{h, t \to t'}$
be the per-token attention mass to the concept span at a chosen
reference layer $\ell^\star$. The AGRS attention-weighted activation
rate is

\[
r^+_{\ell, e}
\;=\;
\frac{\sum_t a^+_t \cdot R^+_{\ell, t, e}}
     {\sum_t a^+_t},
\qquad
\Delta_{\ell, e} \;=\; r^+_{\ell, e} - r^-_{\ell, e}
\]

with $a^- \equiv 1$ on the unprefixed prompt. The decode-time
intervention is identical to \textsc{SteerMoE}: add $\pm \alpha$ to
the top-$k_+$ positive and top-$k_-$ negative experts' router logits.
\textsc{SteerMoE} is recovered exactly when $a^+_t \equiv 1$.

\paragraph{Pipeline.}
Per-token router logits and attention weights are captured via
Hugging Face forward hooks, sliced to the shared statement-body
target span, aggregated into the risk-difference table, used to build
a sparse plan, and re-injected at decode time. Generations are scored
by an LLM judge (GPT-4o as primary, Claude as cross-check) on a
$0$--$2$ ordinal scale for thematic concept transfer, with a
human-rater subset of $200$ cases for judge calibration. Statistical
reporting is paired bootstrap $95\%$ CIs across seeds and
paired $t$-tests on seed-collapsed deltas.

\paragraph{Mechanistic analysis.}
For each selected expert $e$ at layer $\ell$, estimate
$M_{\ell, e} = \mathbb{E}_x\!\left[\nabla_x g_{\ell, e}(x)\,
\nabla_x g_{\ell, e}(x)^\top\right]$ of the gating function over the
contrastive prompt pool, and report the cosine between its top
eigenvector and the concept-prototype embedding direction. The
empirical question is whether AGRS picks better-aligned experts than
\textsc{SteerMoE}.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Every experimental step is a Jupyter notebook with a \texttt{papermill}
parameters cell, run identically on a laptop in smoke mode and on the
MIT ORCD cluster via a paired \texttt{sbatch} wrapper. All
generation calls are at temperature $0$ with explicit seeds; every
model load asserts a recorded Hugging~Face revision SHA; every run
writes a \texttt{provenance.json} with the git SHA, cluster job id,
and wallclock. The container is a single Apptainer image; the AGRS
hypothesis is pre-registered on OSF with the decision gate before
the gate-determining experiment runs.

\paragraph{Models and compute.}
Mixtral~8x7B-Instruct (8-bit with CPU offload), OLMoE 1B-7B-Instruct,
Qwen2-MoE 14B, and DeepSeek-MoE-Lite if licensing allows; total
budget $\sim 305$ GPU-hours on the MIT ORCD \texttt{mit\_normal\_gpu}
partition over the academic year.
