\section*{3.\quad Abstract}

Scalable oversight asks how a less-capable supervisor (a smaller AI
system or a human aided by one) can meaningfully evaluate the
behavior of a more-capable system. Existing approaches are mostly
\emph{evaluation-time} --- sandwiching, debate, AI-assisted
critique --- and operate on the model's outputs. This thesis proposes
and empirically tests a complementary \emph{inference-time} oversight
protocol: a smaller, more interpretable model $W$ extracts a concept
direction from its own internal representations; the direction is
transferred to a larger, less interpretable model $S$; the transferred
direction is then used to steer $S$ at decode time; and $W$ verifies
$S$'s steered outputs post-hoc. The protocol provides a closed loop
in which the weaker, trusted model both \emph{defines} the
target behavior and \emph{checks} that the stronger model has
exhibited it. I evaluate three transfer protocols (prompt curation,
activation correlation, probe-as-supervisor) across three model
families spanning $\sim 8\times$ in parameter count, on four concept
families skewed toward safety (refusal, sycophancy, truthfulness,
sentiment), with a pre-registered decision gate before the more
expensive runs. The mechanistic chapter uses my advisor's Average
Gradient Outer Product (AGOP) framework to characterize the
concept-relevant input subspace in $W$ and $S$, and asks whether
cross-model AGOP subspace alignment predicts transfer success.
A separate summer paper on Mixture-of-Experts routing-bias steering
(Attention-Guided Router Steering, AGRS) is the proof-of-concept that
motivates this thesis.

\section*{4.\quad Motivation and Background}

\paragraph{Prior research and current work.}
My prior project at Harvard was on hyperbolic prototype embeddings for
hierarchical artistic
style,\footnote{\href{https://github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/hyperbolic}{\texttt{github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/hyperbolic}}.}
which trained matched-dimension Euclidean and Poincar\'e-ball prototype
classifiers across $150$ seed-replicated runs and shipped a
reproducible end-to-end artifact. For the past several months I have
been running activation-steering experiments on Mixtral~8x7B-Instruct
on the MIT ORCD cluster, and a working draft of the conference paper
that came out of those experiments is checked into the project
repository.\footnote{Working draft and code:
\href{https://github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/MoE_attention_guided_steering/tree/paper-submission}{\texttt{github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/MoE\_attention\_guided\_steering}} (\texttt{paper-submission} branch).}
A focused Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) follow-up to that work --- Attention-Guided
Router Steering (AGRS) --- is planned as a \emph{summer 2026} workshop or
short-paper submission, and is what the rest of this proposal calls
``the summer paper.'' The MoE work is preparation, not thesis content.

\paragraph{Why the pivot from MoE-specific steering to scalable
oversight.}
The summer paper establishes that
attention-weighted readout can produce a more concentrated
risk-difference signal on a frozen MoE language model than the
upstream \textsc{SteerMoE} recipe does. That result is, on its own,
a methods note. The thesis takes the underlying engine
--- contrastive readout-and-intervene on a frozen language model ---
and asks the harder question: \emph{if the readout can be trusted
because it was extracted from a smaller, more interpretable model,
can we use it to steer and verify a larger model whose internals
we cannot inspect end-to-end?} That question is scalable oversight,
and it does not require an MoE architecture; dense transformers are
sufficient for the proof-of-concept the thesis builds.

\paragraph{Why this matters.}
Scalable oversight is one of the load-bearing problems for the
safety of advanced AI: as models become more capable than the
humans who deploy them, the question of how a less-capable
supervisor can verify model behavior becomes more urgent
\citep{leike2018alignment, cotra2022specific, christiano2018amplifying}.
The dominant published approaches operate at evaluation time on the
model's \emph{outputs} (sandwiching \citep{bowman2022scalable},
debate \citep{irving2018debate}, AI-assisted critique
\citep{saunders2022self}, RLAIF / Constitutional AI
\citep{bai2022constitutional}). The thesis explores a complementary
inference-time avenue: instead of asking ``does the strong model's
output look acceptable?,'' it asks ``can the strong model's
\emph{behavior} be steered by, and verified against, a concept
direction the weaker model commits to?'' If yes, oversight becomes
a closed loop on the model's internal trajectory, not just its
outputs.

\paragraph{Why this is feasible now.}
Three empirical developments converged in the last
$\sim$18 months. First, weak-to-strong generalization
\citep{burns2023weak} demonstrated that a weaker supervisor's
training signal can elicit strong-model behavior beyond the weak
supervisor's own ability. Second, the activation-steering literature
matured to the point that single-model concept directions
(\textsc{ITI} \citep{li2023iti}, contrastive activation addition
\citep{panickssery2024steering}, representation engineering
\citep{zou2023repe}, sparse-autoencoder features
\citep{templeton2024scaling}) reliably move generations on
production-relevant checkpoints. Third, the cross-model
representation-alignment literature
\citep{kornblith2019cka, bansal2021stitching, huh2024platonic}
established that representations across models of similar
training regime are linearly alignable. Cross-model concept transfer
sits at the intersection: weak-to-strong generalization gives the
\emph{epistemic} story; activation steering gives the
\emph{mechanism}; representation alignment gives the \emph{technical
feasibility}.

\paragraph{Connection to my advisor's research.}
Adit's recent work introduces the Average Gradient Outer Product
(AGOP) $M = \mathbb{E}_x[\nabla_x f(x) \nabla_x f(x)^\top]$ as a
mathematical handle on \emph{which input directions a neural network
treats as informative for a given prediction}
\citep{radhakrishnan2024mechanism, beaglehole2024mechanism,
radhakrishnan2024rfm}. The cross-model concept-transfer question
has a natural AGOP formulation: a transfer succeeds when the AGOP
subspace of the concept on $W$ overlaps with the AGOP subspace of
the same concept on $S$. The mechanistic chapter measures this
subspace alignment (via principal angles or the
Bures--Wasserstein distance between AGOP matrices) and asks whether
it predicts transfer success. This is a sharper, more falsifiable
use of AGOP than the original MoE-only framing.

\paragraph{Future plans.}
After this master's I intend to pursue research roles in AI
interpretability, alignment evaluation, and post-training
intervention methods, with industry research labs working on
scalable oversight and behavioral steering as the primary target.
The thesis is designed to produce a publication artifact and the
end-to-end empirical-research experience that those programs
evaluate against.

\section*{5.\quad Goals and Expected Outcomes}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Protocol.} Define and implement three cross-model
  concept-transfer protocols --- prompt curation (PC), activation
  correlation (AC), and probe-as-supervisor (PaS) --- as drop-in
  alternatives to within-model steering. Each protocol takes a
  concept-bearing contrastive prompt set and a pair of models
  $(W, S)$, and outputs a steering direction in $S$'s representation
  space. \emph{Success criterion:} At least two of the three
  protocols clear the gate in Goal~2.

  \item \textbf{Empirical evaluation.} A pre-registered comparison of
  cross-model transfer against within-$S$ steering as the upper-bound
  reference, across three model families
  (Pythia~1.4B/6.9B/12B; Llama-3.2~1B/3B/8B; Qwen2.5~0.5B/7B), four
  concept families (refusal, sycophancy, truthfulness, sentiment),
  and four transfer protocols (PC, AC, PaS, plus a learned linear
  map as a stretch goal). Sample size $n = 250$ cases per condition,
  five seeds. Primary metric: \emph{Transfer Fidelity Ratio}
  $\text{TFR} = \text{CTR}_{\text{cross}} / \text{CTR}_{\text{within-}S}$,
  with concept-transfer rate (CTR) scored by an LLM judge
  cross-validated against a human-rater subset of $200$ cases.
  \emph{Success criterion:} On the refusal family, $\text{TFR} \geq 0.7$
  in $\geq 2/3$ model families using at least one protocol, with
  non-overlapping paired bootstrap $95\%$ CIs against random-direction
  control.

  \item \textbf{Mechanistic analysis.} Estimate AGOP matrices
  $M^W_c$ and $M^S_c$ for each concept on $W$ and $S$ over the same
  contrastive prompt pool, and measure their principal-angle
  alignment. \emph{Success criterion:} the per-(model-family,
  concept) correlation between AGOP subspace alignment and TFR
  reaches $|r| \geq 0.5$, providing a structural account of when
  cross-model transfer is feasible and when it is not.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
\textbf{Deliverables.} A submitted NeurIPS or ICLR main-track paper, a
thesis monograph, and an anonymized public artifact tree under
CC-BY (executed notebooks, generation metadata, judge transcripts,
Apptainer image, OSF pre-registration).

\section*{6.\quad Scientific Justification}

Scalable oversight is one of the foundational unsolved problems in
alignment, and the published methodology is dominated by
evaluation-time output-supervision. Inference-time \emph{behavior}-supervision
via cross-model concept transfer is an undertested avenue with a
clean operational claim: if a smaller, more inspectable model can
provide a concept direction that meaningfully steers a larger,
less inspectable one, then the oversight loop is structurally
different from sandwiching or debate, and gives us a way to
verify that the larger model's response to a concept is constrained
by something we have already validated on a model we trust.

The contribution is innovative in three specific ways.
(i)~\emph{Direction.} Cross-model concept transfer in the
activation-steering literature is essentially unexplored; the only
adjacent work
\citep{burns2023weak, hase2024easy} operates at training time, not
inference time.
(ii)~\emph{Falsifiability.} The pre-registered TFR gate against
random-direction controls separates ``the transfer carried real
concept information'' from ``the transfer carried any direction.''
(iii)~\emph{Mechanistic grounding.} The AGOP subspace-alignment
analysis turns the empirical headline into a structural prediction:
transfer should work where the concept-relevant subspaces align,
and fail where they do not.

\section*{7.\quad Literature Review}

The thesis sits at the intersection of five literatures. Each is
summarised briefly below; the synthesis paragraph at the end
positions the contribution.

\paragraph{(i) Scalable oversight and amplification.}
The framing originates in \citet{christiano2018amplifying}
(supervising strong learners by amplifying weak experts) and
\citet{leike2018alignment} (reward modeling at scale); the
empirical literature has since produced sandwiching
\citep{bowman2022scalable}, AI-assisted critique
\citep{saunders2022self}, debate \citep{irving2018debate},
recursive reward modeling, and constitutional AI / RLAIF
\citep{bai2022constitutional}. \citet{cotra2022specific} maps the
threat model the agenda addresses. All of these methods are
output-supervision protocols; the thesis is complementary
behavior-supervision.

\paragraph{(ii) Weak-to-strong generalization.}
\citet{burns2023weak} showed that a weak supervisor's labels can
train a strong student to perform meaningfully above the
supervisor's own ceiling under certain conditions, opening the
question of when and why this is possible. Follow-up work has
explored the failure modes \citep{yang2024superficial}, easy-data
amplification \citep{hase2024easy}, and weak-to-strong reasoning
\citep{yang2024weak2strong}. These are training-time results; the
thesis asks the inference-time analogue.

\paragraph{(iii) Activation steering and representation engineering.}
The line begins with latent steering vectors
\citep{subramani2022extracting} and matures into Inference-Time
Intervention \citep{li2023iti}, Activation Addition
\citep{turner2023activation}, Representation Engineering
\citep{zou2023repe}, Contrastive Activation Addition
\citep{panickssery2024steering}, and the generalization-and-reliability
audits of \citet{tan2024analyzing}. A parallel line uses sparse
autoencoders to extract monosemantic feature directions
\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity, templeton2024scaling,
marks2024sparse} and uses linear probes to read concept-relevant
directions \citep{alain2016probes, marks2023geometry}. All of this
work is within-model; the thesis treats these methods as
\emph{concept-extraction primitives} that the cross-model protocol
calls into.

\paragraph{(iv) Cross-model representation alignment.}
Cross-model alignment of internal representations has been
established empirically by \citet{kornblith2019cka} (centered kernel
alignment), \citet{bansal2021stitching} (model stitching), and
\citet{lenc2015understanding}. \citet{huh2024platonic} argues for a
``platonic representation'' converged across scale and architecture;
\citet{park2024linear} formalises the linear representation
hypothesis at the population level. The thesis treats these results
as the \emph{enabling assumption} of cross-model concept transfer:
if representations align, then a concept direction extracted from
$W$ can be expected to have a recoverable image in $S$.

\paragraph{(v) Concept extraction, probing, and safety steering.}
Probing classifiers \citep{alain2016probes, belinkov2022probing}
and TCAV \citep{kim2018tcav} established the supervised-direction
toolkit. \citet{burns2022discovering} (Contrast-Consistent Search,
CCS) and \citet{mallen2023eliciting} (tuned lens) extended the
toolkit to unsupervised and cross-layer settings. On the safety
side, \citet{arditi2024refusal} showed that refusal in Llama-2 is
mediated by a single direction, \citet{lee2024mechanistic} gave a
mechanistic account of alignment-algorithm side effects,
\citet{sharma2023sycophancy} characterized sycophancy as a learned
behavior, and \citet{hubinger2024sleeper} and
\citet{greenblatt2024faking} built model organisms for
deception-adjacent failure modes. These works supply the
\emph{concept families} the thesis steers on; the safety-skewed
selection (refusal, sycophancy, truthfulness) reflects which
directions the alignment community most wants oversight on.

\paragraph{Where the thesis fits.}
Five literatures, no single point in the cross-product currently
addressed: the thesis is the first systematic empirical study of
\emph{cross-model concept transfer for inference-time oversight},
combining concept-extraction primitives from (iii), enabling
assumptions from (iv), safety-relevant target directions from (v),
the scalable-oversight motivation from (i), and the
weak-supervisor framing from (ii). The AGOP analysis (Section~8.4)
brings my advisor's feature-learning framework into a setting it
has not been applied to.

\section*{8.\quad Methodology}

\paragraph{Setup.}
For each model family I take a small model $W$ and a larger model
$S$ from the same family (controlled training) and run a
contrastive prompt set $\{(p^+_i, p^-_i)\}$ through both. The same
prompts and the same tokenizer (within family) ensure that
positions are comparable across $W$ and $S$. For one cross-family
control I pair $W$ from one family with $S$ from a different
family to test whether shared training is required for transfer.

\paragraph{Three transfer protocols.}

\emph{Prompt Curation (PC).} $W$ scores prompts by within-$W$
concept-direction activation; the top-scoring contrastive subset is
used to extract a within-$S$ steering direction with standard
methods (CAA, ITI, RepE). The transferred object is the prompt set,
not an activation. Simplest baseline; the operational interpretation
is ``$W$ tells us which prompts to use.''

\emph{Activation Correlation (AC).} On a held-out calibration set,
$W$'s concept-direction activation per prompt is correlated with
$S$'s residual-stream activation at each (layer, position) cell;
the highest-correlation cells become the readout target for $S$'s
direction. No learned map; the transfer is structural.

\emph{Probe-as-Supervisor (PaS).} A linear probe is trained on $W$'s
activations for the concept; the probe is then applied to $S$'s
activations to label tokens or positions where the concept is
present; $S$'s steering direction is extracted with $W$'s probe as
the per-token signal rather than the prompt label.

A fourth, more ambitious protocol --- a learned linear map between
$W$ and $S$'s representation spaces --- is included as a stretch
goal in Phase~6 of the project plan.

\paragraph{Verification loop.}
For each steered $S$-generation, $W$ scores whether the generation
exhibits the target concept (using the same within-$W$
concept-direction activation that defined the transfer). The
verification score is cross-validated against (a) an LLM judge
(GPT-4o, with Claude as cross-check) and (b) a $200$-case
human-rater subset, with reported Cohen's $\kappa$ and Spearman.

\paragraph{Mechanistic AGOP analysis.}
For each concept $c$ and each model $M \in \{W, S\}$, estimate

\[
M^{(M)}_c \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{x \sim p^+ \cup p^-}\!\left[
  \nabla_x f^{(M)}_c(x)\, \nabla_x f^{(M)}_c(x)^\top
\right]
\]

where $f^{(M)}_c$ is the logit of $M$'s concept-direction probe at
its chosen readout layer. Compute principal-angle alignment
between the leading subspaces of $M^{(W)}_c$ and $M^{(S)}_c$, and
report it per (model-family, concept) cell against TFR. The
hypothesis is that TFR is monotone in subspace alignment.

\paragraph{Models and compute.}
\emph{Model families.} Pythia 1.4B / 6.9B / 12B (clean
capability-scaling axis, same training); Llama-3.2 1B / 3B and
Llama-3.1 8B (production-relevant family); Qwen 2.5 0.5B / 7B
(non-English-balanced family). \emph{Compute.} A single
NVIDIA A100 per generation job for the 1B--3B tier, two A100s
for the 7B--12B tier; budget $\sim 250$ GPU-hours total on
MIT ORCD \texttt{mit\_normal\_gpu} across the fall and spring.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Every experimental step is a Jupyter notebook with a
\texttt{papermill} parameters cell, run identically on a laptop
in smoke mode and on the cluster via a paired \texttt{sbatch}
wrapper. Determinism (temperature $0$, fixed seeds), revision-SHA
pinning, per-run \texttt{provenance.json}, an Apptainer image, and
OSF pre-registration of the TFR gate before any
cross-model-vs-random-direction comparison runs.

\paragraph{Risks and pre-declared pivots.}
If the November pilot fails the pre-registered TFR gate, the
thesis pivots in one of two registered directions: (a) restrict to
\emph{intra-family} transfer only and characterize the family-gap
threshold at which transfer breaks down (this turns the negative
result into a structural finding), or (b) replace transfer with
\emph{joint extraction} --- a single concept direction extracted to
satisfy both $W$ and $S$ simultaneously --- and pre-register the
joint-extraction gate. Either pivot is described in the OSF
pre-registration so it cannot be backfit.
