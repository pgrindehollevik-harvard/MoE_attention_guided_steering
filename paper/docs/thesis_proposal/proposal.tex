\section*{3.\quad Abstract}

Sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) language models such as Mixtral~8x7B
route every token to a small subset of feed-forward experts via a
learned router. Recent work (\textsc{SteerMoE}) showed that the router
itself is a viable intervention site: by comparing routing on
contrastive prompt pairs we can identify experts whose activation rate
differs in the presence of a target concept and bias routing toward
them at decode time. In a faithful re-implementation I find that this
recipe transfers \emph{weakly} to Mixtral on a question-only
concept-transfer test, and that the underlying signal is diffuse: the
top twenty $(\text{layer}, \text{expert})$ cells out of $32 \times 8 =
256$ account for less than ten percent of the total
$|\Delta_{\ell,e}|$ mass. This thesis proposes \textbf{Attention-Guided
Router Steering (AGRS)}, a drop-in replacement for \textsc{SteerMoE}
in which the per-token contribution to the readout is weighted by the
attention mass that token sends toward a concept-bearing prefix span.
AGRS reduces to \textsc{SteerMoE} when the attention weights are
uniform, which makes the contribution isolatable from confounding
implementation choices. I evaluate AGRS against \textsc{SteerMoE} and
three hidden-state steering baselines across four MoE checkpoints and
four concept families, with a pre-registered Phase-3 decision gate
($\Delta\text{CTR} \geq +0.10$) before the more expensive
generalization runs. The mechanistic chapter uses the Average Gradient
Outer Product (AGOP) framework developed in my advisor's recent work
to ask whether AGRS's expert selections align with concept-distinctive
feature directions in a way \textsc{SteerMoE}'s do not. Deliverables
are a NeurIPS-track method paper, an open-source replication artifact
(Apptainer image, pinned model SHAs, full judge transcripts), and a
written monograph integrating the empirical headline with the
feature-learning lens.

\subsection*{4.2\quad Why this project}

For the past several months I have been running empirical experiments
on \textsc{SteerMoE}-style routing-bias interventions on
Mixtral~8x7B-Instruct on the MIT ORCD cluster. A working draft of the

repository.\footnote{Working draft:
\texttt{paper/docs/paper/main.pdf} in
\href{https://github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/MoE_attention_guided_steering/tree/paper-submission}{%
\texttt{github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/MoE\_attention\_guided\_steering}}
(\texttt{paper-submission} branch).} That draft documents a specific
limitation of the upstream method, which is the empirical hook the
thesis turns on.

Three converging observations made this the project I want to spend
my thesis year on:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Steering is the cleanest empirical wedge into the question of
  \emph{what features a model has learned}. If we can move a
  generation toward a concept by adding a bias to a small set of
  scalar parameters at inference time, then those parameters carry the
  concept in a behaviorally-meaningful sense; the steering experiment
  is a causal probe of representation.
  \item Sparse MoE language models are an unusually clean substrate for
  that probe. Every layer's routing decision is a discrete,
  inspectable choice over a small expert pool, unlike the continuous
  residual stream of a dense transformer. The routing layer thus gives
  steering research a structurally different intervention site than
  any of the existing dense-transformer methods.
  \item Existing steering methods read off the contrastive signal
  using fixed token positions or unweighted averages over a span.
  Attention-guided readouts have been demonstrated for hidden-state
  interventions on dense transformers but never for router-logit
  interventions on sparse MoE models. The gap is identifiable, and
  closing it is the natural next contribution.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{4.3\quad Preliminary results}

The empirical hook for the thesis is concrete. Across $25$
question-only test cases ($5$ fear concepts $\times$ $5$ evaluation
prompts) on Mixtral~8x7B-Instruct, swept across eight
$(\alpha, k_+, k_-)$ configurations of \textsc{SteerMoE}, three things
hold consistently:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The \emph{concept-token hit rate} ---the fraction of cases
  whose response contains any token of the target concept name ---
  moves by at most $0.04$ between baseline and \textsc{SteerMoE}.
  Most configurations show zero shift.
  \item Response length contracts monotonically with the steering
  coefficient $\alpha$, from $-2.16$ words at $\alpha = 1$ to $-5.52$
  words at $\alpha = 0.01$ with a large $k_+$, indicating the
  intervention has a measurable but defocusing effect.
  \item The per-(layer, expert) risk-difference signal that drives the
  \textsc{SteerMoE} plan is \emph{diffuse} on Mixtral. The top twenty
  cells out of the $256$ total capture less than $10\%$ of the total
  $|\Delta_{\ell,e}|$ mass --- there is no small subset of experts
  that explains most of the prefix-conditioned vs.\ unprefixed routing
  gap.
\end{enumerate}

The three observations together motivate AGRS: if the readout is
diffuse and the intervention barely moves the model, then the readout
is the bottleneck, and reweighting it by attention to the concept
span is the lightest-touch method change that could plausibly fix the
diffusion.

\subsection*{4.4\quad How this project fits my advisor's research}

Adit's recent work --- \emph{Mechanism for Feature Learning in
Neural Networks} (\textit{Science}, 2024), \emph{Mechanism of Feature
Learning in Convolutional Neural Networks} (arXiv:2309.00570), and
\emph{Linear Recursive Feature Machines Provably Recover Low-rank
Matrices} (arXiv:2401.04553) --- introduces the \textbf{Average
Gradient Outer Product (AGOP)} as a mathematical mechanism by which
neural networks acquire features. The AGOP matrix
$M = \mathbb{E}_x\!\left[\nabla_x f(x)\nabla_x f(x)^\top\right]$
encodes \emph{which input directions a model treats as informative}
for a given prediction.

For sparse MoE routing the analogous question is: \emph{which input
directions does a router's gating function for expert $e$ at layer
$\ell$ treat as informative}? Adit's framework gives a principled,
non-empirical handle on that question through the AGOP of the gating
network. The mechanistic chapter of the thesis estimates
$M_{\ell, e}$ for selected experts and asks whether AGRS chooses
experts whose top AGOP eigenvector aligns with concept-prototype
directions, and whether \textsc{SteerMoE} does not. This is the
specific intellectual bridge to the advisor's existing toolkit and
the reason this project is the right project to pursue under his
supervision rather than under a generic ML-systems advisor.

  tests that include a property test: \textit{AGRS reduces to

  cases. Success criterion: AGRS lifts CTR by $\geq 0.10$ over
  \textsc{SteerMoE} on Mixtral / fears with non-overlapping paired
  bootstrap $95\%$ CIs, and produces a positive CTR shift in
  $\geq 3/4$ models and $\geq 3/4$ concept families.

  \item \textbf{Goal 3 --- Mechanistic analysis with AGOP.} Estimate
  the AGOP of selected experts' gating functions over the

  selected experts have systematically higher cosine alignment
  between their top AGOP eigenvector and the concept-prototype
  embedding than \textsc{SteerMoE}-selected experts on Mixtral /
  fears, with the per-cell alignment-vs-CTR correlation across
  (model, concept) cells reaching $|r| \geq 0.5$.
\end{itemize}

\section*{6.\quad Scientific Justification}

\paragraph{Why this is interesting.}
Behavioral steering at inference time is one of the few corners of
mechanistic interpretability where small, tractable interventions on
frozen models produce measurable, causal behavioral effects. Most
published steering methods operate on the residual stream of dense
transformers. Sparse MoE models give us a structurally different
intervention site --- the router logit --- whose causal effect is
mediated through a discrete, inspectable selection over a small
expert pool. Whether the readout-and-intervene paradigm transfers
from dense to sparse architectures, and whether it does so with the
\emph{same} readout rules, is an open empirical question with direct
implications for how alignment researchers deploy steering on
open-weight MoE checkpoints (which now make up a large fraction of
the open-weight frontier).

\paragraph{Why this is innovative.} Three innovations distinguish
the proposed thesis from existing work.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Method.} AGRS is, to my knowledge, the first
  attention-guided readout developed specifically for router-logit
  interventions on sparse MoE models. The reduction from AGRS to
  \textsc{SteerMoE} (under uniform attention) makes the contribution
  isolatable from confounding implementation differences --- the
  same intervention site, the same generation pipeline, the same
  experiment harness, the same evaluation. \emph{Only the readout
  changes.}
  \item \emph{Mechanistic framing.} I use Adit's AGOP machinery to
  formalize the feature directions that selected experts respond to,
  then test whether AGRS's selections align with those directions on
  the same concept-prompt pairs. This is a use of the feature-learning
  toolkit on a behavioral-intervention problem where it has not been
  applied; the result either validates the feature-learning lens on
  routing decisions or constrains where the framework applies.
  \item \emph{Empirical rigor.} The evaluation is pre-registered with
  a Phase-3 decision gate ($\Delta\text{CTR} \geq +0.10$ over
  \textsc{SteerMoE} on the Mixtral / fears pilot) before the more
  expensive cross-model and cross-concept phases run. The full prompt
  set, judge transcripts, and seed sweeps are released under CC-BY at
  submission.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why now.} The empirical conditions for this thesis exist
in a way they did not 18 months ago: open-weight Mixtral, OLMoE, and
Qwen2-MoE checkpoints are all released and stable; LLM-as-judge
evaluation with frontier models has become defensible enough to
cross-check with smaller human-rater subsets; and reproducibility
norms in the alignment community (pre-registration, full artifact
release) have matured to the point that a master's thesis can credibly
ship a reviewer-grade reproducibility bundle. The window for a clean,
narrow, reproducible MoE-steering contribution is open.

\section*{7.\quad Literature Review}

I group the literature into four threads. The thesis sits at the
intersection of the third and fourth: it extends sparse-MoE
intervention with a readout machinery taken from dense-transformer
attention-guided steering, and it analyzes the result using
feature-learning theory.

\paragraph{(i) Activation steering on dense transformers.}
The seminal example is \textit{Inference-Time Intervention} (Li et
al., NeurIPS 2023), which adds a learned direction to a small set of
attention heads to reduce hallucination on TruthfulQA.
\textit{Representation Engineering} (Zou et al., 2023) generalizes
the readout-and-edit problem to contrastive prompt pairs at the
residual stream. \textit{Activation Addition} (Turner et al., 2023)
projects the contrastive signal across multiple residual layers via
projected gradient descent. \textit{Attention-Guided Steering} (Tan
et al., 2024) refines \emph{where} on the residual stream to read by
weighting positions by attention mass; this is the direct upstream
inspiration for the readout half of AGRS. None of these methods
target sparse MoE routing.

\paragraph{(ii) Sparse Mixture-of-Experts language models.}
\textit{Mixtral of Experts} (Jiang et al., 2024) and \textit{OLMoE}
(Muennighoff et al., 2024) released the open-weight checkpoints used
in this thesis. Each sparse layer routes every token to two experts
via a learned router. \textit{Qwen2-MoE} (Yang et al., 2024) and
\textit{DeepSeek-MoE} (Dai et al., 2024) round out the
cross-architecture set. The discrete routing decision is the
structure behavioral steering exploits, and routing-pool size
(8--64 experts) is one of the axes we expect AGRS's gain to vary
along.

\paragraph{(iii) Steering sparse MoE models.}
\textsc{SteerMoE} (2024) is the upstream work that first proposed
biasing router logits to alter expert selection at decode time. Its
readout uses uniform averaging over a target span. The diagnostic
results in my preliminary draft show this readout produces a diffuse
risk-difference signal on Mixtral and correspondingly small
behavioral shifts under a question-only test. The thesis builds
directly on \textsc{SteerMoE}'s intervention site while replacing its
readout; the same paper-rule parameters ($\varepsilon = 0.01$,
$k_+ = 20$, $k_- = 0$) are kept as a sanity-check baseline.

\paragraph{(iv) Feature learning and AGOP.}
Radhakrishnan et al.\ (\textit{Science}, 2024) introduce the
\emph{neural feature ansatz} and the AGOP as a mechanism through
which neural networks acquire features. The CNN follow-up
(arXiv:2309.00570) extends AGOP to convolutional architectures, and
the Recursive Feature Machines paper (arXiv:2401.04553) gives
RFM-based recovery guarantees for low-rank structure.
\textit{Quadratic Models for Understanding Neural Network Dynamics}
(ICLR 2024) uses quadratic surrogates for training-dynamics
analysis. The mechanistic chapter of the thesis uses AGOP to
characterize which input-token directions a Mixtral expert's gating
function is sensitive to, and asks whether AGRS's selections align
with those directions.

\subsection*{8.2\quad The AGRS readout, formally}

For a contrastive prompt pair $(p^+, p^-)$ with concept-bearing
prefix span $\mathcal{C} \subset p^+$, let

\begin{itemize}[label={}]
  \item $R_{\ell, t, e} \in \{0, 1\}$ --- indicator that expert $e$ is
  selected by top-$k$ routing at layer $\ell$, token position $t$;
  \item $A^{\ell^\star}_{h, t \to t'}$ --- attention weight from
  token $t$ to token $t'$ at reference layer $\ell^\star$, head $h$;
  \item $a_t \;=\; \max_h \sum_{t' \in \mathcal{C}}
  A^{\ell^\star}_{h, t \to t'}$ --- per-token attention mass
  directed toward the concept span at $\ell^\star$.
\end{itemize}

The AGRS attention-weighted activation rate on $p^+$ is

\[
r^+_{\ell, e}
\;=\;
\frac{\sum_{t \in \text{target}} a^+_t \cdot R^+_{\ell, t, e}}
     {\sum_{t \in \text{target}} a^+_t},
\]

with $r^-_{\ell, e}$ defined identically on $p^-$ with $a^- \equiv 1$
(uniform, since $p^-$ has no concept span). The per-cell risk
difference is $\Delta_{\ell, e} = r^+_{\ell, e} - r^-_{\ell, e}$.
The decode-time intervention is identical to \textsc{SteerMoE}: add
$+\alpha$ to selected positive experts' router logits, $-\alpha$ to
selected negative experts'. \textsc{SteerMoE} is recovered when
$a^+_t \equiv 1$, which makes the contribution isolable.

\subsection*{8.3\quad Computational pipeline}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Routing-trace and attention collection.} For each
  $(p^+, p^-)$, capture per-token, per-layer router logits and
  attention weights via Hugging Face forward hooks. Slice to the
  shared statement-body target span.
  \item \emph{Risk-difference table.} Compute $\Delta_{\ell, e}$
  under both the AGRS and \textsc{SteerMoE} readouts on the same
  traces. The contrast (top-$k$ share of $|\Delta|$ mass under each
  readout) is one of the headline diagnostics of the final paper.
  \item \emph{Sparse plan selection.} Globally select the top
  $k_+$ positive cells and top $k_-$ negative cells, subject to
  $|\Delta_{\ell, e}| > \varepsilon$.
  \item \emph{Decode-time intervention.} Add $\pm\alpha$ to selected
  experts' router logits at every forward pass during generation.
  \item \emph{Evaluation.} Score each generation with an LLM judge
  (GPT-4o by default, Claude as cross-check) on a $0$--$2$ ordinal
  scale for thematic concept transfer. Cross-validate against a
  $200$-case human-rater subset (Cohen's $\kappa$, Spearman). Report
  paired bootstrap $95\%$ CIs over seeds and paired $t$-tests on
  seed-collapsed deltas.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{8.4\quad Mechanistic analysis with AGOP}

For a selected expert $e$ at layer $\ell$, treat the expert's gating
function $g_{\ell, e}(x)$ as a real-valued map from token-position
embeddings to the pre-routing logit, and estimate

\[
M_{\ell, e} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_x\!\left[
  \nabla_x g_{\ell, e}(x)\, \nabla_x g_{\ell, e}(x)^\top
\right]
\]

over the contrastive prompt pool. Compare the top eigenvectors of
$M_{\ell, e}$ against the concept-prototype embedding direction. The
empirical question: \emph{do the experts AGRS selects respond to
feature directions that an AGOP-style analysis identifies as
concept-relevant, or does AGRS find them incidentally through the
activation-rate differential alone?}

\subsection*{8.5\quad Reproducibility infrastructure}

The thesis adopts a single, strict reproducibility pattern that is
implemented in the project's
\href{https://github.com/pgrindehollevik-harvard/MoE_attention_guided_steering/blob/paper-submission/notebooks/README.md}{%
\texttt{notebooks/}} layer:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The notebook is the pipeline.} Each experimental
  step is a Jupyter notebook that declares its inputs as a
  \texttt{papermill} \texttt{parameters} cell, calls into a
  pure-function entrypoint in
  \texttt{src/.../pipelines/}, runs identically on a laptop (CPU,
  smoke mode) and on the MIT ORCD cluster (GPU, full mode) via a
  paired \texttt{slurm/notebooks/<name>.sbatch} wrapper, and
  archives the executed copy with full outputs into the run
  directory. No experimental result lands in \texttt{experiments/}
  that did not come from a \texttt{papermill}-executed notebook.
  \item \textbf{Determinism.} All generation calls use temperature
  $0$, top-$p$ $1.0$, with explicit seeds threaded through every
  pipeline function.
  \item \textbf{Pinning.} A helper asserts the recorded
  Hugging~Face revision SHA on every model load; the assertion
  fires before any compute.
  \item \textbf{Provenance.} Every run writes
  \texttt{experiments/<run\_dir>/provenance.json} with the git SHA
  of the codebase, the cluster job id, the hostname, the wallclock,
  and the resolved papermill parameter values.
  \item \textbf{Containerization.} One Apptainer image, built from
  \texttt{containers/agrs.def}, is the only supported cluster
  runtime.
  \item \textbf{Pre-registration.} The AGRS hypothesis (primary
  metric, comparator, $n$, seeds, stop rule, Phase-3 decision gate)
  is registered on OSF before any AGRS-vs-\textsc{SteerMoE}
  comparison runs.
  \item \textbf{Public release.} An anonymized
  \texttt{experiments/} tree under CC-BY ships at submission so a
  reviewer can re-derive every figure offline.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{8.7\quad Risks and contingency}

\paragraph{Phase-3 decision gate.} If AGRS does not clear the
pre-registered $\Delta\text{CTR} \geq +0.10$ threshold on
Mixtral / fears, the thesis pivots in one of two pre-declared
directions: (a) the same attention-weighted readout is applied to
a hidden-state intervention site, isolating the readout from the
intervention site; or (b) the thesis becomes a focused
diagnostic-and-AGOP study of why the routing signal is diffuse on
Mixtral. Either pivot is registered in the OSF pre-registration so
it cannot be backfit.

\paragraph{Cross-model port engineering.} Each new MoE checkpoint
requires a backend module analogous to the existing Mixtral one.
The plan budgets one engineering week per model, with
DeepSeek-MoE-Lite as the most fragile (storage, license); if it
stalls, the thesis reports three models rather than four and the
decision is documented in \texttt{checkpoint\_card.md}.

\paragraph{Refusal-family release.} The refusal-target concept
family carries dual-use considerations; we engage with the
institutional contact early in Phase~6 and ship a public-release
go/no-go decision before the family's generations enter the paper.

\paragraph{AGOP estimation cost.} AGOP requires gradients of the
gating function through tens of thousands of tokens. Phase 7
budgets $\sim 20$ GPU-hours; if tight, AGOP is estimated at a
single representative layer rather than all $32$.
